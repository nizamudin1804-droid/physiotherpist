Lumbar Spine Recovery: 4–6 Wks

Lumbar Disc Bulge & Herniation (L4-L5, L5-S1)

Inner nucleus pulposus protrudes through weakened outer disc rings, compressing spinal thecal sacs or descending nerve roots.

Primary Symptoms:

Sharp lower back ache, electric buttock pain, numbness in big toe or foot, pain aggravated by coughing.

Physiotherapy Approach:

McKenzie directional preference exercises, manual pelvic traction, neurodynamic flossing, and deep multifidus recruitment.

Nerve Entrapment Recovery: 3–5 Wks

Sciatica & Piriformis Impingement

Irritation or entrapment of the sciatic nerve beneath a tight, hypertonic piriformis muscle in the deep gluteal space.

Primary Symptoms:

Deep burning ache in buttocks radiating down the posterior hamstring to the calf; unbearable during car driving.

Physiotherapy Approach:

Biomedical dry needling to piriformis, sciatic neural glides, sacrococcygeal mobilization, and hip rotator strengthening.

Cervical Spine Recovery: 3–4 Wks

Cervical Radiculopathy & Postural Text Neck

Nerve root compression in the cervical vertebrae (C5-C7) caused by prolonged forward-head posture and facet arthrosis.

Primary Symptoms:

Neck stiffness, shoulder blade pain, pins-and-needles down the arm into the thumb/index finger, grip weakness.

Physiotherapy Approach:

Manual cervical traction, deep neck flexor motor retraining, thoracic extension mobilization, and ergonomic coaching.

Headache Clinic Recovery: 2–4 Wks

Cervicogenic & Tension-Type Headaches

Referred pain originating from the C1-C3 cervical spine segments and hypertonic suboccipital musculature wrapping over the skull.

Primary Symptoms:

Dull, band-like tightness around forehead, pain radiating from base of skull behind one eye, aggravated by neck posture.

Physiotherapy Approach:

Upper cervical SNAGs (Mulligan technique), suboccipital trigger point release, dry needling, and posture retraining.

Shoulder Joint Recovery: 6–8 Wks

Rotator Cuff Tears & Supraspinatus Tendinitis

Degenerative wear or acute trauma to the supraspinatus, infraspinatus, or subscapularis stabilizing tendons of the glenohumeral joint.

Primary Symptoms:

Pain arc between 60° and 120° of arm abduction, nighttime throbbing when sleeping on shoulder, inability to lift arm overhead.

Physiotherapy Approach:

Radial Shockwave therapy (ESWT) for tendon remodeling, scapulothoracic strengthening, and eccentric rotator cuff drills.

Capsular Care Recovery: 8–12 Wks

Adhesive Capsulitis (Frozen Shoulder)

Progressive thickening, fibrosis, and contracture of the glenohumeral joint capsule, causing severe global loss of movement.

Primary Symptoms:

Severe loss of passive and active external rotation, inability to fasten a bra or reach for back pocket, acute nighttime ache.

Physiotherapy Approach:

High-grade capsular distraction mobilizations, Class IV deep laser to break fibrosis, and progressive end-range home pendulums.

Sports Knee Phased: 6–9 Mos

ACL Tear & Meniscus Pathology

Rupture of the anterior cruciate ligament or fibrocartilage meniscus tear during high-velocity pivoting, cutting, or hyperextension.

Primary Symptoms:

Audible 'pop' at moment of injury, rapid knee effusion (swelling) within 2 hours, knee buckling/giving way on stairs.

Physiotherapy Approach:

Pre-hab swelling reduction, post-operative quad activation (NMES), hamstring graft protection, and force plate hop clearance.

Patellofemoral Recovery: 4–6 Wks

Patellofemoral Pain Syndrome (Runner's Knee)

Abnormal lateral tracking of the patella within the femoral trochlear groove, creating excessive retropatellar cartilage stress.

Primary Symptoms:

Dull anterior kneecap ache aggravated by descending stairs, running, deep squats, or sitting through long movies ('theater sign').

Physiotherapy Approach:

Gluteus medius and VMO targeted strengthening, IT band myofascial unloading, McConnell patella taping, and gait retuning.

Foot & Heel Recovery: 4–6 Wks

Chronic Plantar Fasciitis & Heel Spurs

Micro-tears and chronic degenerative collagen degeneration at the calcaneal insertion of the thick plantar aponeurosis.

Primary Symptoms:

Excruciating stabbing heel pain with the first steps out of bed in the morning; dull ache returning after prolonged standing.

Physiotherapy Approach:

Extracorporeal Shockwave Therapy (ESWT), high-load toe-elevated calf raises, night splints, and custom orthotic foot mapping.

Ankle Tendon Recovery: 6–8 Wks

Achilles Tendinopathy (Mid-Portion & Insertional)

Failed healing response characterized by collagen disorientation and hyper-vascularity in the thickest tendon in the human body.

Primary Symptoms:

Morning tendon stiffness, localized nodular thickening 2–6 cm above heel, painful push-off during walking or running.

Physiotherapy Approach:

Alfredson eccentric loading protocol, heavy-slow resistance (HSR), shockwave therapy, and running cadence optimization.

Upper Extremity Recovery: 3–5 Wks

Lateral Epicondylalgia (Tennis Elbow)

Overuse tendinopathy of the extensor carpi radialis brevis (ECRB) origin, common in desk workers, racquet sports, and tradespeople.

Primary Symptoms:

Point tenderness on the outer bony elbow knob, burning pain when gripping coffee mugs, weak wrist extension against resistance.

Physiotherapy Approach:

Mulligan Mobilization with Movement (MWM), Tyler Twist eccentric wrist extensor bar exercises, and dry needling.

Hip Joint Recovery: 6–8 Wks

Femoroacetabular Impingement (FAI) & Labrum

Mechanical friction between the femoral head and acetabulum rim (cam or pincer morphology), pinching the stabilizing hip labrum.

Primary Symptoms:

Deep groin pinching during low squats or prolonged seated posture; pain with internal hip rotation ('C-sign' hand grip).

Physiotherapy Approach:

Manual inferior hip distraction glides, deep pelvic rotator motor retraining, avoiding impingement arcs, and core stability.

Clinical Red Flag Notice: When to Seek Immediate Emergency Hospital Care

While physiotherapists excel at treating musculoskeletal conditions, certain signs suggest emergency medical issues (e.g. Cauda Equina Syndrome, vascular occlusion, or fractures). Please proceed directly to the nearest A&E / Emergency Department if you experience:

  • • Sudden loss of bowel or bladder control
  • • Numbness in the saddle area (inner thighs/groin)
  • • Rapid progressive bilateral leg paralysis or foot drop
  • • Severe unremitting night pain with fever & chills
